## Your §3.1 — already normative, before your reply arrived

Specified in `oim-taxonomy-derived.md`, §"Matching a result to a binding":

> A result **applies to** a binding when its `cubeName`, `networkName` and
> `total` are those of the binding, and every aspect in its `aspects` has the
> same value in the binding. Where more than one applies, **the one recording
> the most aspects applies**. A result constraining fewer aspects is a verdict
> on a *different* binding, not a looser opinion on this one.

That last sentence is yours — it says why the rule is the right resolution
rather than an arbitrary tiebreak, which the original text did not. Also added:
`oimde:ambiguousCalculationResult`, forbidding a producer from publishing two
results that apply to one binding with equal specificity. The Arelle emitter
already satisfies it, 0 violations on the Microsoft filing.

One measurement note, since our numbers differ and the record should be right. I
counted **17 of 184** results matched by a more specific sibling, of which **1**
genuinely disagrees; you counted 11 of 183 disagreements. Almost certainly
different things — one ambiguous result fans out across several more-specific
siblings, so display-level disagreements exceed result-level ones. The
conclusion is identical and the spec cites the figures I can reproduce.

## Your §3.2 — done, as a model-defined cube type

The cube now says what it is. A translated model carries:

```json
"cubeTypes": [ { "name": "msft:legacyAccommodationCubeType",
                 "baseCubeType": "xbrl:reportCube" } ],
"cubes":     [ { "name": "msft:legacyAccommodationCube",
                 "cubeType": "msft:legacyAccommodationCubeType", … } ]
```

**Match on the cube's `cubeType` local name, `legacyAccommodationCubeType`**, and
drop the cube from a navigator. That replaces the group-tree absence you were
relying on, which as you said was incidental.

Three things about the name, since they are the whole point of it:

* **"Accommodation", not "all facts".** The cube exists because a legacy XBRL 2.1
  instance has no notion of cube membership, so a model that requires one has to
  accommodate it. Naming it for what it *contains* invited two misreadings.
* **Nobody should ever author one.** "allFactsCube" read as a feature to adopt.
  Nothing should produce this but the legacy translation.
* **It is not ESEF's `[999999] Line items not dimensionally qualified`.** That is
  a dedicated extended-link role the RTS requires, **authored by the filer** to
  link items needing no dimensional information to a predefined hypercube, and it
  *belongs* in a navigator. Ours is generated by a processor so translated
  calculations have somewhere to bind, and does not. A name built on "all facts"
  or "not dimensionally qualified" would blur exactly that line.

**Why a model-defined type rather than a reserved one.** A reserved
`xbrl:legacyAccommodationCubeType` would give you a single QName to match across
every model, which is a contract rather than a convention — but it needs
specification surface, and the working group's near-term business is getting the
draft into public review. This does not block on that. If a reserved type is
adopted later, this one simply derives from it instead of from `xbrl:reportCube`
and you move from the local name to the QName; nothing about the artifact
restructures.

I also considered and rejected a general "processor generated" property. On the
translated Microsoft model it would mark **129 of 129 groups, 123 of 124 cubes
and 142 of 145 networks** — a legacy DTS is entirely translated, so it would tell
you nothing. Those objects at least correspond to something the filer authored,
an extended-link role or a calculation arc. This cube corresponds to nothing,
which is why it alone is marked.

## Your §4.3 — the producer side is ready

`derivedContent.factValues` carries every resolved value keyed by
`factValueName`, so it resolves one-to-one against your post-split facts. On the
Microsoft filing: 1,827 `resolved`, plus any `bound` from an applied journal,
which supersede a resolved value for the same occurrence — the model's own
sources did not locate it on that surface, which is why it was bound by hand.
